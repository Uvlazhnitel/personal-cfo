# Synthetic Financial Dataset

## Purpose and Ownership

Stage 3 provides a deterministic, test-owned financial history for exercising the complete Stage 2 engine. It contains no personal data and is not a seed, migration, production export, or provider payload. Production domain and engine APIs remain unchanged.

The fixture lives in `packages/financial-engine/test/fixtures/stage3/`:

- `synthetic-financial-life.ts` builds canonical financial facts.
- `synthetic-scenarios.ts` supplies checkpoint context, projections, forecast plans, and controlled variants.
- `synthetic-expectations.ts` stores reviewed literal `bigint` anchors.

Identifiers are UUIDv7 values derived from fixed subsystem/index ranges. Builders use no clock, random source, Faker, mutable ID counter, network, or database.

## Financial History

The primary run is fixed at `2026-09-14T08:00:00Z` (`Europe/Riga` effective date `2026-09-14`). History contains 24 complete calendar months from September 2024 through August 2026 and a partial September 2026.

Three EUR accounts exercise each accepted authority model: Primary Bank uses balance snapshots, Cash Wallet uses the ledger, and Investment Account uses portfolio valuations. Primary salary is €3,200 on the nominal 28th, shifted from Saturday to Friday and Sunday to Monday. Side income is explicitly classified and never opens a Pay Cycle.

Spending includes five recurring and five variable categories, seasonal utilities, summer and December variation, and a legitimate €900 February 2026 outlier. Investment facts include €50 monthly recurring contributions, one €1,000 ad-hoc contribution, and independent positive and negative market movements.

The completed trip fund is allocated in six €200 events and consumed once for €1,200 on 2026-06-12. The current €600 insurance fund has €300 reserved; its current-cycle requirement is €133.34 with €33.34 outstanding. A physical cash count records €140 → €125, followed by an exact reversal and a separately recovered €15 consumption transaction.

Projected Sinking allocation changes protection composition only; the funded external purchase is the sole cash outflow. Forecast assumptions remain €50 monthly investment and €200 monthly cash contributions even when Step-Up is recommended.

Checkpoint operational needs cover the complete exclusive interval from the checkpoint date to the next reliable salary. The fixture enumerates every intersecting calendar month, so late-August and late-September checkpoints include the following month's recurring debits rather than optimistically declaring an incomplete list complete.

## Golden Expectations

Expected values are checked-in constants, not values generated by the engine during the test. Tests reconcile fixture-level monthly totals before asserting engine outputs. The representative current CCR is:

```text
recognized income       €3,350
net consumption           €480
reserve increase          €100
capital created         €2,770
CCR                     277 / 335
```

The healthy fixture must produce a deterministic Step-Up from €50 to €100. Any future failure of that policy anchor requires reviewing the accepted Stage 2 rules; fixture data must not be adjusted merely to conceal a formula change.

It also carries a literal rolling CCR reference for `2026-06-14T08:00:00Z` through `2026-09-14T08:00:00Z`: €9,750 recognized income, €5,095 net consumption, €300 reserve growth, €4,355 capital created, and ratio `67/150`. The latest five Pay Cycles, from 2026-04-28 through the open 2026-08-28 cycle, have checked-in IDs, salary boundaries, closure state, and expected-next-pay data.

## Limitations and Calibration Notes

This is a compact synthetic life, not statistically representative user research. It is EUR-only, assumes complete provider reconciliation except in named variants, and keeps the fulfilled trip fund `active` because Stage 2 has no effective-dated fund-status history. Historical bank snapshots are derived from fixture ledger movements, while checkpoint planning context remains explicit as required by Stage 2G.1.

The high accumulated cash balance intentionally supplies persistent-surplus evidence and makes the healthy Step-Up path unambiguous. Numerical policies—six-month baseline, 36-month lookback, Cash Drag thresholds, four Pay Cycles, €50 contribution step, and 60-day stress horizon—remain candidates for Stage 4 verification and later calibration; this dataset records behavior but does not tune policy.
